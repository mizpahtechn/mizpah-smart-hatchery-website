Chapter 1: Understand what AI can and cannot do

Generative AI can draft, summarise, classify, brainstorm and help structure information. It can also produce confident errors, outdated claims, biased outputs and fabricated references. Treat outputs as drafts, not automatic truth.

Use AI for low-risk assistance where a human can review the result. Keep people responsible for decisions involving money, legal rights, health, safety, employment or sensitive customer data.

Chapter 2: Set up safe and useful workflows

Define the task, audience, inputs, desired format, constraints and review criteria. Provide only the information needed. Remove personal, confidential and commercially sensitive data unless the tool and organisation are approved for that use.

Create a repeatable workflow: prepare source material, prompt, generate draft, verify facts, edit for context, approve and record. Keep a human owner for every published or customer-facing output.

Chapter 3: Research and business planning

Use AI to organise interview notes, generate research questions, compare business-model options and identify assumptions that need evidence. Ask it to separate facts, hypotheses and unknowns.

Verify market size, regulations, competitor claims, prices and citations against primary or authoritative sources. AI-generated market estimates are not a substitute for customer research or financial modelling.

Chapter 4: Marketing and content production

Use AI to outline articles, adapt approved material to formats, draft email variants, generate campaign calendars and improve clarity. Provide brand voice, audience and prohibited claims.

Review every factual statement, link, image right, product claim and call to action. Do not fabricate testimonials, customer stories, endorsements or performance results. Disclose AI use where required or appropriate.

Chapter 5: Sales and customer support

AI can help draft responses, classify enquiries, summarise conversations and prepare follow-up messages. Build a knowledge base from approved policies and product information.

Do not allow an unreviewed system to promise refunds, discounts, delivery dates or legal outcomes. Escalate complaints, payment disputes, safety concerns and unusual requests to a trained person.

Chapter 6: Operations and productivity

Use AI to draft standard operating procedures, checklists, meeting summaries, training materials and project plans. Test the procedure with the people who will use it and correct missing steps.

Keep source-of-truth records in approved systems. AI should not silently modify stock, payments, employee records or customer accounts. Use permissions, audit logs and human approval for consequential actions.

Chapter 7: Data analysis and decision support

AI can help explain a dataset, suggest charts, draft SQL or identify questions for analysis. Check calculations independently and inspect how missing values, duplicates and outliers are handled.

Do not paste sensitive records into unapproved tools. Validate formulas, sample sizes and definitions. Correlation is not causation; ask what alternative explanations could account for a pattern.

Chapter 8: Governance, measurement and improvement

Maintain an inventory of AI use cases, owner, data category, tool, risk, approval and review date. Measure time saved, error rates, customer outcomes and total cost—not just the number of generated outputs.

Review tool terms, retention, training use, security, intellectual property and applicable law. Retire workflows that create more correction work or risk than value. Keep a fallback process when the tool is unavailable.

Your implementation plan

  1. Define the result you want and the baseline you will measure.
  2. Gather current evidence and identify assumptions that need testing.
  3. Choose a small, time-bound experiment with a clear owner and budget.
  4. Review results, customer feedback, cash impact and risks.
  5. Document the decision, update the process and set the next review date.
Practical worksheet: record the objective, action, responsible person, resources, deadline, success measure, evidence and contingency.
