Chapter 1: The purpose of a business plan

A business plan is a decision document. It explains the customer problem, proposed solution, market evidence, operating model, resources, financial logic and risks. A lender or investor needs to understand how the business will generate cash and repay or reward capital.

Start with the decision the plan must support: launch, improve, borrow, invest or expand. Match the level of detail to that decision. Do not confuse a polished presentation with evidence of viability.

Chapter 2: Research the market and customer

Define the target market by customer type, geography, need and buying behaviour. Use credible secondary sources for market context and primary research—interviews, observation, pilots and sales records—for specific demand.

Distinguish total market from the portion you can realistically serve. Document the source, date, method and limitation of each important estimate. Avoid multiplying a large population by an assumed price and calling it a sales forecast.

Chapter 3: Define the offer and competitive position

Explain the product or service, customer outcome, delivery method, pricing logic, service boundaries and evidence of quality. Identify direct competitors, indirect alternatives and the option of doing nothing.

Build a comparison around attributes customers value: price, convenience, reliability, availability, quality, support and trust. State a difference you can actually deliver and defend.

Chapter 4: Design the operating model

Describe the customer journey from enquiry to fulfilment and after-sales support. Map suppliers, premises, equipment, people, technology, inventory, quality controls and critical dependencies.

Estimate capacity using realistic throughput, staffing, downtime and seasonality. Identify permits, standards, insurance and compliance needs. Explain how the operation will continue if a key supplier or asset fails.

Chapter 5: Plan the route to market

Set out channels, sales process, promotional activity, partnerships, customer service and retention. Define the sales assumptions by channel and show the evidence behind conversion rates, order values and repeat purchases.

Create a 12-month marketing and sales calendar with owners and budgets. Track leads, qualified opportunities, conversion, acquisition cost, repeat sales and customer complaints.

Chapter 6: Build credible financial forecasts

Prepare linked assumptions for sales volume, price, variable cost, fixed cost, payroll, capital expenditure, working capital, tax and financing. Build monthly cash flow for at least the first year and annual projections beyond it where appropriate.

Show profit and loss, cash flow and projected balance sheet. Explain break-even, contribution margin, debt service and funding needs. Separate confirmed figures from estimates and label assumptions clearly.

Chapter 7: Assess funding and risk

Specify how much funding is required, when it is needed, what it will pay for and how the business will contribute. Match funding type and tenor to the asset or cash cycle being financed.

Create a risk register with likelihood, impact, early warning, mitigation, owner and contingency. Include demand, price, supply, operations, people, regulation, cyber, climate and cash risks relevant to the venture.

Chapter 8: Write, present and maintain the plan

Use a concise executive summary, but write it last. Organise the plan so a reader can find the market case, operating plan, management, financials, funding request and supporting evidence. Use consistent units and dates.

Before submission, reconcile every number across the narrative, tables and appendices. Remove unsupported claims, identify sensitivities and prepare answers to likely questions. Update the plan when evidence changes.

Your implementation plan

  1. Define the result you want and the baseline you will measure.
  2. Gather current evidence and identify assumptions that need testing.
  3. Choose a small, time-bound experiment with a clear owner and budget.
  4. Review results, customer feedback, cash impact and risks.
  5. Document the decision, update the process and set the next review date.
Practical worksheet: record the objective, action, responsible person, resources, deadline, success measure, evidence and contingency.
