Business Registration, Tax and Legal Compliance in Nigeria

A practical orientation to Nigerian business registration, records, tax administration, employment and compliance checks. Verify current rules before acting.

Chapter 1: Choose a suitable business structure

Common structures include a business name and an incorporated company. They differ in legal personality, governance, liability, ownership, filing and financing implications. The right choice depends on scale, risk, owners and plans.

Review current Corporate Affairs Commission requirements and official guidance before filing. Confirm the proposed name, ownership, persons with significant control where applicable, registered details and post-registration obligations.

Chapter 2: Understand registration and company records

Keep registration documents, constitutional documents where applicable, ownership records, director or proprietor details, resolutions, statutory registers and evidence of filed changes. Restrict access to identity and ownership information.

Update the relevant registry when required. Do not use another person's identity or submit inaccurate particulars. Seek qualified help for complex ownership, foreign participation or regulated activities.

Chapter 3: Understand tax responsibilities

Tax obligations depend on the entity, income, activity, location, employees and current law. Potential areas include income tax, VAT where applicable, withholding tax, payroll-related deductions and other statutory levies.

Maintain transaction records and confirm current filing, payment, threshold, exemption and rate rules with the Nigeria Revenue Service and relevant state authorities. Do not rely on old online summaries or generic tax calculators.

Chapter 4: Keep accounting and evidence

Record sales, expenses, assets, liabilities, payroll, inventory, invoices and receipts. Reconcile bank and payment accounts. Keep source documents in an organised, backed-up system for the retention period required by applicable rules.

Separate personal and business transactions. Document owner loans and drawings. Correct errors transparently and ensure tax returns reconcile with underlying books.

Chapter 5: Employ people lawfully and fairly

Before hiring, define role, pay, working hours, leave, performance expectations, safety and confidentiality. Use written agreements appropriate to the employment relationship and comply with applicable labour requirements.

Where relevant, verify current duties for payroll deductions, pension, employee compensation, workplace safety and other statutory schemes. Do not classify a worker as a contractor solely to avoid obligations.

Chapter 6: Licences, permits and sector rules

Some activities require permits, professional approvals, product standards, environmental approvals, local permits or sector-specific registration. Requirements may differ by state and local government.

Identify the regulators relevant to the actual activity and location. Obtain approvals before operating where required. Keep renewal dates and conditions in a compliance calendar.

Chapter 7: Consumer, privacy and digital obligations

Provide accurate product information, transparent pricing, delivery terms, complaint channels and fair refund processes. For online services, protect personal data, secure systems and explain how customer information is used.

Review the Nigeria Data Protection Act and current guidance from the Nigeria Data Protection Commission. Collect only necessary data, control access, manage processors and prepare a response plan for incidents.

Chapter 8: Create a practical compliance calendar

List each filing, payment, licence renewal, payroll task, board or ownership update and record-retention duty. Record the authority, due date, responsible person, evidence and escalation route.

This guide is educational, not legal or tax advice. Nigerian rules can change and application depends on facts. Verify the current law and official guidance and consult a qualified Nigerian lawyer, accountant or tax practitioner before acting.
