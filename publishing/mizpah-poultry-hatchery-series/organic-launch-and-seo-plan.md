## 1. Publishing position

The series is a practical, research-informed learning collection for poultry farmers, breeder-farm managers, hatchery technicians, hatchery operators, agricultural students and poultry entrepreneurs in different regions.

The five volumes are thematic groupings, not a compulsory reading order. Each title is intended to become a standalone publication with its own editorial scope, book page, metadata, approved cover, sample content and free/paid access decision.

Do not mark a title as published, available, free or paid until its manuscript, technical review, final file, cover, catalogue entry and access route have all been approved and tested.

## 2. Editorial workflow and release gates

1. **Scope:** Define reader, outcomes, species, production system and boundaries; map overlap with the other books.
2. **Research:** Use current primary research and authoritative extension, breeder and equipment documentation. Record source edition and access date.
3. **Draft:** Write original explanations, examples, worksheets, SOPs and troubleshooting logic. Never reproduce proprietary manuals or third-party book text.
4. **Technical review:** Have technical recommendations checked by a qualified poultry/hatchery reviewer. Verify all units, formulas, thresholds and caveats.
5. **Editorial review:** Check originality, clarity, terminology, references, accessibility and consistency.
6. **Production:** Format accessible e-book files; insert the exact user-approved cover artwork without redesign or cropping.
7. **Catalogue setup:** Create the individual book page, metadata, access rules, pricing and related links.
8. **QA and release:** Test page on mobile and desktop, verify checkout/download permissions, confirm canonical URL and sitemap entry, then publish.
9. **Post-release:** Monitor reader questions, corrections, page performance and source updates.

## 3. International technical writing rules

- Use metric units as primary; add Fahrenheit conversions where they help readers.
- State species, strain, machine type, measurement method and production context whenever they change the recommendation.
- Do not present forced-air and still-air incubator settings as interchangeable.
- Distinguish wet-bulb readings from relative humidity; do not silently convert one into the other.
- Avoid one-size-fits-all incubation or egg-storage rules. Explain the limits of each reference and require equipment-manufacturer and strain-specific guidance.
- Identify jurisdiction-dependent veterinary, vaccination, welfare, biosecurity and trade requirements.
- Clearly distinguish fertility, hatchability of eggs set, hatchability of fertile eggs, saleable chick rate and chick mortality.
- Use worked examples as examples, not universal performance targets.
- Date-stamp technical reviews and revise content when authoritative guidance changes.

## 5. Website requirements for every released book

- A stable, descriptive URL under the existing Knowledge Centre / Digital Library architecture.
- Unique page title, meta description, canonical URL and social sharing metadata.
- One clear H1 and logically nested section headings.
- Original summary, intended readers, learning outcomes, detailed contents and sample excerpt.
- Exact approved cover image with descriptive alt text; preserve the original aspect ratio.
- Accurate author/imprint, edition, publication date, language, format and ISBN only when actually assigned.
- Clear free/paid status, price, currency, access terms and working purchase/download path.
- Links to relevant books, free articles and courses.
- Accessible text contrast, keyboard navigation, responsive layout and descriptive link text.
- Appropriate Book/Product structured data only when the page truly represents an available publication. Do not add fake ratings, offers, ISBNs or availability.
- Sitemap entry only when the page is public and useful to readers.

The collection landing page is already added to the existing sitemap. Individual book pages should be added only at release.

## 6. Organic launch programme

### Website and search
- Publish useful, original articles that answer real poultry and hatchery questions.
- Use a single canonical source page for each book and link supporting articles to it.
- Add contextual internal links from the poultry blog, Knowledge Centre, Digital Library and relevant product/service pages where appropriate.
- Submit the sitemap and released URLs through Google Search Console after verifying property access.
- Monitor indexing and technical errors; do not promise indexing or rankings.

### YouTube
- Produce short demonstrations and longer explanatory lessons.
- Give each video a precise title, useful description, captions and links to the relevant article or book.
- Use original footage, diagrams or licensed media.

### Facebook and Instagram
- Publish concise educational posts, carousel explainers, diagrams, excerpts and short videos.
- Participate helpfully in relevant poultry communities; follow each group's rules.
- Avoid repetitive promotional posts and unsupported claims.

### TikTok and short-form video
- Convert one researched topic into a short, self-contained lesson.
- Prioritise practical questions, demonstrations and common mistakes.
- Link to a relevant article or catalogue page where platform features allow.

### WhatsApp Business and email
- Share new resources with opted-in readers and customers.
- Use clear descriptions and direct links; do not add people to broadcast lists without permission.
- Maintain a simple editorial/newsletter cadence and unsubscribe process where applicable.

### Partnerships
- Approach poultry associations, agricultural educators, veterinary professionals, hatchery operators and relevant training organisations.
- Offer useful educational contributions and clearly disclose any commercial relationship.

## 7. Suggested 12-week organic rollout

| Period | Deliverables |
|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | Series landing page; complete Book 1 research and technical scope; create editorial templates and source register |
| Weeks 3–4 | Draft and review Book 1; prepare 2–3 supporting educational articles and first video outlines |
| Weeks 5–6 | Complete Book 1 editing and production; build and QA its individual catalogue page; publish only after approval |
| Weeks 7–8 | Publish related free articles and videos; collect reader questions; start Book 2 manuscript |
| Weeks 9–10 | Review search queries, indexing, page engagement and reader feedback; improve existing content |
| Weeks 11–12 | Prepare the next release only if it passes all gates; publish an organic performance report |

This is a sequencing guide, not a promise that a technical book can be completed within a fixed number of days. Accuracy takes priority over release speed.

## 8. Organic performance dashboard

Track monthly:
- Search impressions, clicks, click-through rate and average position by page and query.
- Indexed versus excluded URLs and crawl issues.
- Organic sessions, country/region, engaged visits and referral sources.
- Free downloads, paid orders and conversion rate.
- Email/WhatsApp opt-ins and unsubscribes.
- YouTube views, watch time and referral visits.
- Social reach, saves, shares, comments and qualified catalogue visits.
- Corrections, technical questions and reader feedback.

Do not treat follower count or raw views as sales. Attribute purchases to the relevant landing page or campaign link where tracking consent and platform rules permit.

## 9. Advertising and privacy controls

- Initial launch is organic only.
- Do not create or activate Google, Meta, TikTok or YouTube paid campaigns.
- Do not spend money, install new tracking pixels or share customer lists with advertising platforms without separate approval and appropriate privacy review.
- Do not promise guaranteed hatch rates, disease prevention, income or business results.
- Obtain permission before using reader testimonials, photographs, email addresses or user-generated content.
